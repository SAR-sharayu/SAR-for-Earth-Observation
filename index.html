Why Active Microwave Radar?

How Satellites Image Earth with SAR

Unlike passive optical cameras that rely on sunlight and are blocked by clouds or smoke, Synthetic Aperture Radar transmits its own microwave pulses (3 cm to 70 cm wavelength) and records both backscatter amplitude and coherent phase day and night.

01 • VIRTUAL APERTURE

Doppler Beam Sharpening

By coherently combining radar echoes along the satellite's 7.5 km/s orbital path, a 10-meter antenna synthesizes a virtual aperture several kilometers wide.

02 • MILLIMETER PHASE

Interferometry (InSAR)

Comparing phase differences between two satellite passes (Δφ = 4πΔr/λ) reveals millimeter-scale fault slip, volcano inflation, and urban subsidence.

03 • MULTI-BAND PENETRATION

X, C, L & P Wavelengths

Short X/C-bands scatter off building roofs, ocean waves, and crops, while longer L/P-bands pierce through forest canopies to map trunks and ground motion.

Spaceborne SAR Missions at a Glance

Sentinel-1 (ESA Copernicus) C-Band • 5.6 cm

250 km TOPSAR Interferometric Wide swath with 6–12 day global repeat for tectonic faults, subsidence, floods, and sea ice.

NISAR (NASA–ISRO) L + S Band • 24 & 9.4 cm

Dual-frequency SweepSAR penetratating heavy vegetation to track Himalayan & San Andreas deformation, glaciers, and biomass.

ALOS-2 / ALOS-4 (JAXA) L-Band • 23.6 cm

Quad-polarimetric L-band PALSAR for tropical deforestation alerts, forested landslide creep, and volcanic unrest.

TerraSAR-X / TanDEM-X (DLR) X-Band • 3.1 cm

Sub-meter spotlight resolution and bistatic single-pass interferometry for global DEM generation and urban infrastructure.

EOS-04 / RISAT-1A (ISRO) C-Band • 5.6 cm

All-weather hybrid polarimetric radar supporting monsoon crop monitoring, hydrology, soil moisture, and flood mapping.

BIOMASS (ESA) P-Band • 70 cm

Ultra-long 70 cm wavelength polarimetric & tomographic radar piercing dense rainforest canopies to map global forest carbon.
